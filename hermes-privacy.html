Privacy Policy for Hermes

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Hermes is a personal software tool built and used by one person, Joe Green. It runs on a private server he controls, behind his own password. It is not a public service and it has no other users.

What the app does

At its owner's direction, Hermes can connect to the owner's own Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive accounts, using Google's sign-in consent (OAuth). The owner signs in with Google directly; Hermes never sees or stores the owner's Google password. Hermes can read mail, calendar events, and files. It can send mail, create or edit calendar events, and upload or edit files only after the owner approves the exact action. Hermes cannot delete mail, events, or files — no delete function exists in it.

What data is stored, and where

Sign-in tokens issued by Google are stored only on the owner's own server. Mail, calendar, and file content is fetched when the owner asks for it and is not kept beyond what the task needs. Hermes contains no advertising, no analytics, and no tracking.

AI processing

Hermes works by sending the owner's requests, and the content those requests involve, to the AI service the owner has chosen and pays for (currently DeepSeek), so that the service can carry out the work. Content is never sold, and it is never shared for anyone else's purposes.

Google user data

Hermes's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Apart from the AI processing described above, Google user data is not transferred to anyone.

Removing access

The owner can disconnect Hermes at any time from inside the app, which deletes the stored tokens from his server, and can also revoke the app's access at any time from the Google Account permissions page.
